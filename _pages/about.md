I am an Assistant Professor in the Department of History at North Carolina Central University, where I teach African American history, U.S. history, and digital humanities. My work sits at the intersection of Black historiography, digital archival practice, and public history methodology.

My current book project, *Locating Black Folk*, develops a framework I call Black Cultural Reality — an approach to African American history grounded in African-derived philosophy. I am also completing a book proposal, *"Lest Our Hearts... We Forget Thee": Essay in Black Cultural Reality Theory*, and working on a project on money, saving, and Black economic life in twentieth-century Baltimore.

Earlier in my career I served as a CLIR Postdoctoral Fellow in African American Studies at the University of North Carolina at Greensboro, where I worked on the *People Not Property* slave deeds project and served as project manager for *No Longer Yours: Slavery and Freedom in North Carolina*. I remain an advisory board member for UNCG's Digital Library on American Slavery.

I hold a Ph.D. in History from the University of South Carolina and both an M.A. and B.A. in History from North Carolina Central University.

Research interests
======
- African American history and Black philosophy of history
- Slavery and freedom in the American South
- Nineteenth-century U.S. educational and cultural history
- Digital humanities, public history, and digital archival practice
